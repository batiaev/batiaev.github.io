Options

14 pages

The concepts every strategy assumes, then one page per structure — each with a live payoff chart you can edit and open in the calculator.

  • Concepts

  • What an option actually is

    Calls, puts, strike, expiry and the difference between intrinsic and extrinsic value — the vocabulary everything else assumes.

  • The Greeks, without the calculus

    Delta, gamma, vega and theta as four questions about the same position, and which one actually kills you.

  • Implied volatility is the price

    Why an option's quoted premium is really a volatility number, and what the smile is telling you.

  • Strategies

  • Long call

    The simplest bullish option: defined risk, unlimited upside, and a bill for time decay every day you are wrong.

  • Long put

    Downside exposure with a floor on the loss — the cleanest way to be short without unlimited risk.

  • Covered call

    Own the underlying, sell someone the right to take it from you above a set price, and keep the premium either way.

  • Cash-secured put

    Get paid to bid below the market, with the cash set aside to buy if you are filled.

  • Bull call spread

    Buy a call, sell a higher one. Cheaper than the call alone, with the upside capped at the short strike.

  • Bear put spread

    The same trade pointed down: buy a put, sell a lower one.

  • Long straddle

    Buy the call and the put at the same strike. A bet on movement, indifferent to direction.

  • Short strangle

    Sell an out-of-the-money put and call. Collect premium while nothing happens — and carry an undefined tail.

  • Iron condor

    A short strangle with both tails bought back. Same idea, defined risk, smaller credit.

  • Long butterfly

    A cheap, narrow bet that the underlying finishes at a particular price.

  • Collar

    Hold the asset, buy a protective put, and sell a call to pay for it.

  • VAR, and what it does not tell you

  • DV01 and the shape of rate risk

  • Dynamic hedging, and where the premium goes

    Sell an option, hedge it to expiry, and watch the premium leave through the hedge — why the price you collect is the price of the gamma you are short.

AI engineering

4 pages

  • The harness is most of the product

    A model is one component. The context it is given, the tools it may call, the permissions around them and the checks after them decide whether an agent is useful.

  • Agentic workflows that hold up

    Plan, act, verify — and why the verify step has to be something the model cannot talk its way past: tests, type-checks, property checks, and a human at the irreversible step.

  • Knowledge bases: typed beats retrieved

    Why retrieval over a pile of documents fails quietly, what hybrid search fixes, and why goals, decisions and artefacts work better as typed nodes with provenance.

  • Product engineering when code is cheap

    When an agent can write the diff, the scarce work moves to the spec, the verification and the taste encoded in rules — and the line between product and engineering moves with it.
